# The data boundary and Veeam (proposal)

Security context: this brief is defensive security engineering under the charter in `CLAUDE.md`. It concerns what may leave the operating organisation's own CMOS estate and
in what form.

The owner (2026-10-25 09:42Z and 09:44Z), in short: backups have their own archival and encryption, and that is enough until a requirement forces CMOS to wrap Veeam's encryption or roll its own. Veeam stays a viable path **as long as the data in it is never part of the CMOS system**,
because anything that enters CMOS never leaves unless it is permitted to, and permitted data must be off-boarded. **Data that can leave and be used independently of CMOS to create or recreate CMOS as data can never be permitted to leave.**
And what CMOS owns is narrow: the files on a file server and the databases of the in-house systems are **not** CMOS; CMOS only locks the access to them and offers the interface, and **the interface stores no data**. What CMOS stores is information it generates for itself.

## Three kinds of data

| Kind | Examples | CMOS owns it? | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Company data** | file-server files, the databases of the in-house systems, mailboxes | **No.** It stays where it is. CMOS owns the access, not the thing | Veeam and every other backup of it are outside CMOS and as today. The warehouses are an I/O layer: they hold no copy that persists. A cache or staging area holds a copy only in memory or for a bounded time and is never the only copy; if one persists to disk it is CMOS information under the next row |
| **CMOS information** | registers, the audit journal, settings, the knowledge base, the state of operations master, Doppler, Milky Way and the warehouses' own indexes and results | **Yes**, generated by CMOS for CMOS | It never leaves unless permitted, and a permitted leaving is an off-boarding with a record |
| **Recreation material** (a part of CMOS information) | the roots and issuing CA keys, token signer keys, the sealed credential store and its key rings, the White Key records, the cold-start vault, Doppler's attestation registers, the warehouses' data keys | Yes | May never be permitted to leave in usable form. It exists inside CMOS and in the **named copies** (the vault copies, the archival disk). **The two roots' key material is the exception: it exists only on the White Keys** (owner, 10:01Z) |

Two consequences the owner should see. First, **the stale-key release** ([ceremony-and-staleness.md](ceremony-and-staleness.md)) hands over the locks and the CMOS information of the warehouses; the operating organisation's files and databases are not touched, because they were never in CMOS. What the new deployment re-takes is the access lock, not the data. Second, **the unavoidable exception**: a cold start needs recreation material to exist outside the running system, otherwise nothing could restart it. The named copies are that exception, under the White Key rules.

## The question: Veeam backups of CMOS VMs and SQL hold CMOS information

A Veeam restore point of a CMOS VM or of the CMOS SQL data is a copy of CMOS information in a repository outside CMOS (and replicated to the DR site). By the rule that is a leaving, and for the VMs that carry recreation material it is one that may never be permitted.

### What has to be found out about the live estate

The questions below are answered from the live backup system by reads only, never by writing, and recorded per deployment.

| Question | How to find out |
|---|---|
| Does the backup product cover CMOS VMs? | the jobs and the VMs and machines each covers; flag every operations master, Doppler, Milky Way, warehouse and CA VM |
| Is the CMOS SQL data in a backup? | whether the VM that holds the database is in a job, and whether a restore drill exists for it |
| Is the backup encrypted? | the encryption setting per job, where the password is kept, whether the repositories and replicas are encrypted |
| Are the roots in the backup? | **No (the owner's rule): the two root CAs exist only as certificates and on the White Keys.** This replaces the earlier plan (ca-plan 4) that stored each root image on the backup server, at the DR site and in the vault. |

### The read-only checks to run first (by a thread with access, on the owner's OK)

The operations master's backup connector can answer these with reads only (it uses GET and the token request). No read has been made yet.

1. The jobs and which VMs and machines each covers; flag every operations master, Doppler, Milky Way, warehouse and CA VM.
2. For each job: whether encryption is on, and where the password is recorded.
3. Each repository's location and whether it is encrypted; the replication and copy targets (the DR site).
4. Whether any copy of a root or CA VM exists in any repository. After the owner's rule of 10:01Z there must be none: if there is one, it is deleted by a gated step and the finding goes to the run record.

### The roots live only on the White Keys: what that changes

The owner (10:01Z): the only other place the two root CAs exist, besides as certificates, is on the White Keys. Consequences, all for the owner to confirm:

1. **No copy elsewhere.** The images, the `certutil` key backups and the vault wraps of ca-plan 4 go away. What a root needs to exist again is the key material on the keys and the repository (the VM is rebuilt from the recipe at every ceremony). The vault keeps passwords and tokens, never a root.
2. **The minting ceremony (owner, 10:03Z), the ritual that binds the Lighthole Council (LHC, owner 10:13Z).** A minting ceremony creates the two CAs with their root CAs and the needed configuration, then isolates the CAs completely, shuts them down, encrypts them and stores **verified, identical copies on all White Keys**. Once CMOS has verified that this state exists on every White Key, it goes into productive mode (the owner wrote "EOS"; read as CMOS). So the keys hold whole, identical copies, not different shares, and productive mode waits for the all-keys check. The check is a gate on arming (ca-plan 1b) and a line in the run record.
3. **Single-holder risk, still for the owner to answer.** An identical copy on every key means one holder with a key and its PIN could, if the copy opens with the PIN alone, sign as a root alone. That would break "a new CA certificate needs a majority" and "never the single origin of a permanent write". Recommendation that keeps the owner's picture: **every key holds the same sealed copy, and the key that opens it is split three of five** (or opens only when three holders present their PINs in one ceremony), so the copies stay identical and verifiable by hash, and a root can be used only with a majority. If the owner prefers a copy that opens with one key, the ceremony stays one-admin as in ca-plan 4 and the single-holder risk is accepted in writing. Costs of the recommendation: the yearly root-CRL ceremony needs three holders together, and losing the unsealing threshold loses a root (CS-05).
4. **Catalogue.** CS-02, CS-03 and CS-05 change: the offline roots are not a separate survivor, and the White Keys are the only place a root survives. CS-03 (people gone) is where the five keys and the three-holder threshold bite hardest.
5. **P3 check C7** names the White Keys as the only named copy for a root; a root key found anywhere else fails the run.

## Effects on the cold-start criteria

- P3 gets a seventh check, C7: **no recreation material is outside its named copies in usable form**, run by the assistant's Prove step against the register.
- P2 ("the last backup") names, per dataset, which mechanism it is: company data in Veeam, CMOS state in CMOS's own signed backups, recreation material in the named copies.
- CS-02 (the wiper) improves: CMOS state no longer depends on the online repositories the attacker reaches; CS-08 (the brain is dead) becomes "redeploy from the bundle, then restore signed state".
- The bundle and the vault are, by this rule, the only exits of recreation material; both are encrypted at rest and opening them is a White Key read.
